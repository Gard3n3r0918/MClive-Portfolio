MS Capstone Research

Care Compass

Peer Review Submitted — April 2026

Informatics for Health and Social Care · Submission ID 263298015

Communication Failures and Workload Burden in Patient-Facing Digital Health Platforms: A Mixed-Methods Socio-Technical Analysis and Design Framework

Melissa Clive, Jamie Oviedo, Emily Weeden, Sang-Hwan Kim · University of Michigan-Dearborn, IMSE

344,733 User Reviews Analyzed
10 Digital Health Platforms Benchmarked
17 Stakeholder Interviews (10 patients, 7 nurses)

Research Overview

Patient-facing digital health platforms—including patient portals—frequently generate communication-related friction that increases patient confusion, anxiety, and clinician workload. Using a convergent parallel mixed-methods design, we analyzed 344,733 user reviews across 10 digital health platforms and conducted semi-structured interviews with 10 patients and 7 nurses.

Competitive benchmarking identified critical unmet needs in Provider Communication and Interaction and Insurance Navigation and Cost Transparency. Both data streams confirmed that inadequate digital design systematically shifts cognitive work to patients and operational work to clinical staff—a persistent socio-technical misalignment.

Phase 1: Large-Scale Review Analysis

  • • 854,858 app store reviews collected across 10 platforms; 344,733 retained after quality filtering
  • • Team used Natural Language Processing (NLP) — automated text analysis — to measure how often users discussed specific features and whether their tone was positive or negative
  • • 7 feature categories scored for coverage, user sentiment, and competitive strength
  • • Benchmarked healthcare apps against best-in-class comparators (Redfin, Credit Karma, Lose It)
  • Programming and data pipeline built by teammate; Melissa contributed to analytic framing, interpretation of results, and design recommendations

Phase 2: Qualitative Interviews

  • • Semi-structured interviews, 45–90 min, conducted via Zoom or in person (Sept–Oct 2025)
  • • 10 patient participants (age 28–78) and 7 registered nurses (≥2 years experience)
  • • Thematic analysis used to identify patterns across interviews — categorizing recurring experiences into meaningful themes
  • • Findings integrated with Phase 1 results to build a complete picture of where and why current digital health tools fall short
  • Melissa led: interview design & facilitation, empathy-driven participant engagement, thematic coding, journey mapping, and Care Compass conception

Six Critical Friction Points Identified

Thematic analysis of patient interviews revealed six sequential friction points, corroborated by quantitative review analysis.

FP 1 · Patient
"Initial Shock"

Patients receive abnormal test results without context, generating acute anxiety before they can speak with a provider.

FP 2 · Patient
"Interpretation Chaos"

Medical jargon and abbreviations in clinical notes leave patients feeling excluded from their own care and unable to make informed decisions.

FP 3 · Patient
"Forgot to Ask"

Patients enter appointments unprepared and remember critical questions only after leaving, wasting limited provider time.

FP 4 · Provider
"Clinical Time Drain"

Nurses spend substantial unscheduled time explaining decontextualized portal results — reactive education that could be proactively supported by the system.

FP 5 · Provider
"Post-Visit Backlog"

Portals become a "second visit" — nurses face 20+ daily messages from patients seeking post-appointment clarification that should have been preempted.

FP 6 · Both
"Advocacy Roadblocks"

Systemic insurance opacity, billing confusion, and fragmented information force patients into advocacy roles they are unprepared and unsupported to fill.

The Care Compass Framework

Proposed Design Framework

Synthesizing both study phases, we propose Care Compass — a human-centered design approach with three integrated components targeting the identified friction points through explainable AI.

Component 1 — XAI-Powered Medical Term Translator

An Explainable AI overlay that automatically translates medical jargon in released test results and clinical notes into plain-language explanations calibrated to the user's eHealth literacy level — displaying interpretation source and confidence level to support informed clinical conversations. Directly addresses Friction Points 1 & 2.

Component 2 — Smart Appointment Preparation Module

A structured, AI-assisted pre-visit module that prompts patients to enter current concerns, generates 3–5 prioritized provider questions based on recent labs and care history, and surfaces accessible health data summaries. Replaces the blank sheet of paper patients currently forget to use. Addresses FP 3; reduces FPs 4 & 5.

Component 3 — Insurance & Cost Navigation

An AI-driven triage system providing proactive, plain-language explanations of insurance coverage decisions and EOB documents at billing — plus step-by-step advocacy guides for coverage disputes and prior authorization. Targets the largest identified unmet need in the ecosystem. Addresses FPs 5 & 6.

Four Integrated Findings

1

Digital systems amplify health literacy gaps. Current platforms appear designed for users who already possess high health literacy, effectively excluding those who need support most — raising urgent health equity concerns.

2

Inadequate design directly transfers workload to clinical staff. Quantitative review analysis and nurse interviews both confirmed that poor patient-facing features generate reactive, unscheduled provider communication.

3

Cost transparency is the largest unmet need. Low coverage paired with consistent patient interview themes confirmed cost transparency as the most critical, addressable gap in the digital health ecosystem.

4

Socio-technical misalignment is systemic. Current platforms present fragmented, jargon-heavy information without contextual guidance, forcing a massive cognitive burden transfer from system to patient.
